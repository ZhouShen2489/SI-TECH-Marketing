# STYLEGUIDE

## Brand intent

The site should feel like a strong B2B solutions company, not a generic outsourced IT vendor and not a flashy startup landing page.

The tone should communicate:

- clarity
- trust
- execution
- technical seriousness
- practical collaboration

## Voice

### What to do

- Speak to the client’s operating problem directly.
- Prefer concrete workflow language over abstract transformation language.
- Explain what gets better after the work is done.
- Use business-friendly English and straightforward Chinese.
- Make the visitor feel that the team can actually help move the work forward.

### What to avoid

- Avoid empty terms like “empower,” “innovate,” and “redefine” unless they are tied to a specific outcome.
- Avoid writing for internal audiences.
- Avoid placeholder language such as “reserved for future products.”
- Avoid sounding like a translated corporate brochure.
- Avoid large blocks of policy-heavy or headquarters-heavy language on core pages.

## Messaging pattern

Each section should answer one of these:

1. What problem is happening?
2. What does Si-Tech Intl do about it?
3. What kind of team is this for?
4. Why is this credible?
5. What is the next step?

## Content rules

- Prefer specifics such as lead response, service coordination, handoffs, project circles, knowledge continuity, operator support workflows, portals, CRM, dashboards, and integrations.
- If a sentence can be made more concrete, make it more concrete.
- If a claim sounds broad, anchor it in a business scenario.
- If the copy sounds like it is written for ourselves, rewrite it for a client.

## Visual rules

- Primary look: deep blue, clean neutrals, restrained warm accent.
- Keep generous whitespace and large image areas.
- Use cards only when they help scannability; do not let the whole site become a card grid.
- Favor split sections, strong hero backgrounds, and full-width bands.
- Stories should open and expand instead of sitting as shallow tiles.
- Product and solution visuals should feel related in color and tone, even if the content differs by section.

## Page behavior

- Home should feel like the strongest narrative page.
- Solutions should route clearly into detail where detail matters.
- Stories should help a visitor understand the work quickly through concrete situations.
- Contact should feel structured and trustworthy, not like a generic email box.

## Repository rules

Every repo should keep these files at the top level:

- `README.md`
- `PRD.md`
- `TASKS.md`
- `STYLEGUIDE.md`
